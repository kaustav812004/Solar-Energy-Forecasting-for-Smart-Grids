The global energy transition is accelerating. Solar photovoltaic (PV) capacity surpassed
1 TW worldwide in 2022 and is projected to reach 5 TW by 2030 (1). India, with an
average daily solar insolation of 4–7 kWh/m2, is particularly well-positioned for large-scale
solar deployment; the country targets 500 GW of renewable capacity by 2030 under its
Nationally Determined Contributions. However, solar PV generation is fundamentally
intermittent — it depends on the movement of the Earth relative to the Sun, the optical
depth of the atmosphere, and the stochastic behaviour of cloud cover — making its
large-scale grid integration a formidable operational challenge.
Grid operators must balance supply and demand in real time. When solar generation
is under-forecast, expensive peaking plants must be dispatched on short notice. When
it is over-forecast, curtailment or voltage instability may follow. Day-ahead forecasting
— predicting the full 24-hour generation profile at least 12–24 hours in advance — is
therefore the critical temporal horizon for wholesale market bidding, unit commitment,
and reserve scheduling.
